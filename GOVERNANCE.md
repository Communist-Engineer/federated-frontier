# Governance summary

FFI treats governance as a control plane for decision rights, resource allocation, ownership, audit, conflict resolution, and release authority.

The institutional problem follows the technical one: compute, data, and labor can be socially distributed while weights, trademarks, and deployment power remain concentrated. That arrangement socializes production and privatizes control. A durable federation therefore needs enforceable contributor and public-interest rights across data, compute, models, and governance software.

Current governance status is **Proposed**. The project should compare a mission-locked foundation, cooperative, university consortium, and multi-stakeholder commons before incorporating. Technical integration may remain delegated to a small accountable group while constitutional rights, budgets, safety gates, and asset disposition remain collectively governed.

# Licensing and intellectual property options

The repository already uses the MIT License. This corpus preserves it. Future artifacts require separate, explicit decisions because code, documentation, datasets, model weights, trademarks, patents, and services have different rights.

## Options

- Code/docs: continue MIT for simple permissive reuse, or propose a future change only through owner authorization and an ADR. Existing contributions remain under their accepted terms.
- Data: retain source-level licenses and purpose/jurisdiction constraints in manifests; no umbrella license can overwrite upstream rights.
- Model weights: compare permissive open-weight terms, Open Model/MDW-style terms, responsible-use restrictions, and controlled research access against Open Source AI Definition compatibility and enforceability.
- Contributions: evaluate DCO versus CLA based on provenance, patent, and relicensing requirements.
- Patents: default publication may preserve commons but foreclose some filings; defensive patent commitments require governance.
- Trademark: can protect users from misleading unofficial releases without restricting code study.

Questions include derivative-model status, training-as-use, database rights, publicity/personality rights, international enforcement, export/sanctions, and whether restrictions are legally and technically enforceable. Obtain jurisdiction-specific counsel before accepting high-risk data or releasing large weights.

Recommendation: keep MIT unchanged now; make manifests artifact-specific; defer model-weight terms until Track A model selection and the governance/release process are defined. Document “open weights,” “open source,” and “open development” separately.

# Contributor economics

Contribution accounting must reward verified marginal value, not simply elapsed GPU time. A participant can expend real power on duplicated, stale, poisoned, or policy-ineligible work.

Candidate score:

\[
U_i=C_iQ_iV_iR_iP_i-A_i,
\]

where normalized compute/output `C`, measured marginal quality `Q`, verification confidence `V`, reliability `R`, and policy eligibility `P` are discounted by avoidable network/storage/verification externality `A`. The formula is an auditable accounting model, not a tradable token requirement.

## Mechanisms to test

- Posted jobs with declared acceptance tests and maximum price.
- Cost reimbursement for trusted research islands plus attribution.
- Bounties for machine-verifiable artifacts and independent replications.
- Capacity grants or reciprocal quotas among consortium members.
- Reputation as evidence for scheduling, never as ownership by itself.
- Bonds/escrow only when enforceable recourse justifies complexity.

Donated compute has opportunity, electricity, hardware-wear, administration, and privacy costs. Consumer hardware may be economically best for rollouts/evaluation even when poor for coupled training. Accounting reports rejection reasons and permits disputes without revealing hidden tests.

Contributors must know whether their work confers compensation, attribution, governance voice, model access, intellectual-property rights, or none. Socialized production with concentrated private ownership is not resolved by a leaderboard.
